Understanding temperature conversion

This calculator converts temperatures among Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin for weather, cooking, classroom work and general scientific use. Type the known temperature and select its scale. All three equivalent values update together, which makes it easy to compare common reference points without repeating the calculation.

Temperature formulas

Temperature scales do not all begin at the same zero, so temperature conversion requires both multiplication and an offset. Convert Celsius to Fahrenheit with °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32. Convert Fahrenheit to Celsius with °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9. Convert Celsius to Kelvin with K = °C + 273.15.

Worked examples

  • 20°C to Fahrenheit: (20 × 9/5) + 32 = 68°F.
  • 95°F to Celsius: (95 − 32) × 5/9 = 35°C.
  • 300 K to Celsius: 300 − 273.15 = 26.85°C.

Useful reference points

  • Water freezes at 0°C, 32°F or 273.15 K under standard reference conditions.
  • A comfortable indoor temperature of 20°C equals 68°F.
  • Water boils at about 100°C or 212°F at standard atmospheric pressure; altitude and pressure can change the boiling point.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not convert a temperature difference by adding 32. A change of 1°C equals a change of 1.8°F, but offsets apply only to actual scale readings. Kelvin values use the symbol K without a degree sign. Negative Kelvin values are not physically meaningful because absolute zero is 0 K.

Accuracy and appropriate use

Displayed results are rounded for readability while calculations use greater precision. Kitchen thermometers, weather reports and scientific instruments may have their own measurement uncertainty. For medical, laboratory or industrial safety decisions, use calibrated equipment and the required professional procedure rather than relying only on a web conversion.

Frequently asked questions

What temperature is the same in Celsius and Fahrenheit?

−40°C and −40°F represent the same temperature.

Why does Kelvin start at 273.15?

Zero Celsius corresponds to 273.15 Kelvin because the Kelvin scale begins at absolute zero.

Can I use this for oven temperatures?

Yes, it provides the mathematical conversion. Actual oven temperature can vary, so follow the recipe and appliance guidance.
